CH 01 · METHOD

Five signals. One under-performing player. The same trail every time.

↓ Scroll to read
01
MARKETS

Order books move first.

Spot-fixing shows up on one player's prop first. In Case 095-N, Player N's Map 3 kill line dropped from 15.5 to 13.5 — a −2.0 point move ten minutes before the map.

→ DRIFT
Continuous polling · cross-referenced against that player's historic kill line at the same map.
02
PLAYERS

Performance Index

Every player has a statistical fingerprint — kill rate, damage, deaths, accuracy — measured against his own 90-day baseline. In Case 095-N, Player N collapsed to a 0.61 rating on Ancient, his career-best map (1.19).

→ INDEX
KDR · KPR · ADR · DPR · Shot accuracy — all measured against the player's own baseline, never a tour average.
03
PATTERNS

What the eye cannot follow.

Reaction time, crosshair placement and decision latency are fingerprints a player can't fake under pressure. In Case 095-N, first-contact reaction slowed from 125ms to 218.8ms and crosshair angle drifted +1.7° past his own range — on the exact map the line moved.

→ REFLEX · PHANTOM
Tick-precise reaction timing and crosshair tracking · frame-level demo parsing · all measured against the player's own 90-day window, never a tour average.
04
CHATTER

Someone always tells someone.

Before the map ends, someone has already said it. In Case 095-N, public HLTV and Telegram posts named Player N's under on Map 3 — pointing at a name, not a scoreline.

→ WATCH
Public HLTV, Telegram and Discord monitoring · phrases matched to the flagged player and surfaced before the map ends.
05
GAMEPLAY

Gameplay Analysis

Round-by-round review surfaces what statistics can't explain. In Case 095-N, Player N took 10 disadvantaged peeks into multi-enemy holds on his career-best map.

→ GAMEPLAY
Round-by-round footage · disadvantaged peeks, unused utility and passive positioning matched against the player's own career pattern.
CH 02 · SIGNAL
Live · Detection layer online

The scoreboard says nothing.
One player still threw his line.

Spot-fixing doesn't need the team to lose on purpose — it hides inside a normal result. Win or lose, one player quietly drops under his own kill line: a few rounds of soft peeks, missed utility, deaths that don't fit his record. It rarely starts on the server; it starts on the order book, where his line moves before tip-off. Integra is the detection layer that catches a single under-performing player before anyone else.

Verdict · no evidence of kill-suppression

The opportunity test was built specifically to detect quiet kill-suppression in deniable moments — it returned the opposite.zero winnable deniable duels (z = −1.83, below his own baseline). His low kill count is explained by chances, not choices: 16 winnable duels vs a typical 29 — roughly half. Of the 16 he got, none of his declines were in unwitnessed spots.

Six independent measures — crosshair, reaction time, pass-through, no-react, kill-total/leverage, and this opportunity model — converge on the same reading: a rough game on a map his team lost, not a throw. The only thing that could reopen it is the part a demo can never show: an abnormal betting market on this exact match.
